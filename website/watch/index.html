                      How Lyra Watch works

                      Trades come from SEC EDGAR (Form 4 insider filings and 13F fund holdings) and the US House Clerk's STOCK Act Periodic Transaction Reports, parsed from the filed PDFs. House reports give value ranges, so totals use the bottom of each range. Filings are made days to weeks after the trade (13F up to 45 days after the quarter). The Senate's disclosure site has no open feed and is not covered yet.

                      Statements and coverage come from news headlines (Google News RSS), plus RSS feeds of a person's own accounts where they publish one. X/Twitter and Facebook aren't read: their terms forbid scraping and their data APIs are paid. Company mentions, power-move labels, issues and tone are keyword rules over the headline text. They're a starting point for reading the source, not a verdict.

                      People and popularity. Names are found in headlines by rule: a run of capitalised words that starts with a known given name, or, in sentence-case headlines, two rare capitalised words. A lone surname counts once the full name appears elsewhere in the coverage. Job comes from the watchlist, then from how headlines describe the person ("Nvidia CEO"), then from Wikipedia's one-line description. Popularity is 0 to 100: 70% Wikipedia page views over the last 30 days (log scale, so 1,000 a day is about 60 and 100,000 a day is 100) and 30% headline count. Someone with no Wikipedia article scores on coverage alone, capped at 30.

                      Politics and interests. Party membership and current offices come from Wikidata or an official source and are linked; Lyra never rates or infers anyone's political views. "Political coverage" is the share of a person's headlines about elections, taxes, immigration, trade or policy. Interests are the topics their coverage is about.

                      For AI agents. Every person has a stable JSON record at /api/watch/people/{id}, the latest stories are a JSON Feed at /api/watch/feed.json, and /llms.txt describes it all.

                      Political affiliation is shown only when it's documented: a party membership or office, with a link to the source. Lyra doesn't infer anyone's politics from their words, and doesn't record religion or other sensitive personal traits.
