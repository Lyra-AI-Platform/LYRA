Tremor Index Experimental

The Tremor Index reads a US stock from public data: its price trend, the market's fear gauge (VIX), news headlines, company insiders' trades, large funds' holdings and politicians' disclosed trades. This page explains how it works, shows how it has done so far, and lists every error we have found and fixed.

General information, not financial advice. The Tremor Index is not a recommendation to buy or sell, does not take your circumstances into account, and has not been shown to forecast prices. See the Terms.

Methodology

What a reading predicts

  • Horizon: 30 calendar days, close to close.
  • Boom: the price ends 5% or more higher. Bust: it ends 5% or more lower. Anything in between is flat.
  • Two small logistic models turn the inputs into a boom chance and a bust chance. The lean is bullish when boom exceeds bust by at least 12 points and confidence is at least 30%, bearish in the opposite case, and neutral otherwise.
  • Confidence reflects how many input groups returned data (price 35%, VIX 15%, news 25%, filings 25%), times a factor that grows from 0.6 to 1.0 as the first 50 readings are graded.
  • Exact percentages are hidden until a backtest shows the model beats simple baselines.

Inputs and starting weights

Every input is scaled to roughly −1 to +1, where positive is bullish. These are the hand-set starting weights. After each reading is graded at 30 days, the learner adjusts them slightly towards what happened.

Every reading shows each source's status. If price, news or insider data fails, the reading is marked partial and is not added to the track record.
